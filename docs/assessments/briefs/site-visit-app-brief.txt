Site Visit App Brief: Scopes and Conditional Logic
Oct 13, 2026 · @Jeff Chesebro
Goal and design rules
The app runs a site visit as a short chain of scopes. The assessor picks a visit type, the app switches on the matching scopes, and each answer decides which follow-up questions appear. A question is shown only when an earlier answer makes it relevant, so a venue with no fly system never sees counterweight questions. Every problem found becomes a finding with one ID that carries through quote, repair and verification.
This brief is the product specification for that questionnaire. It draws on the site visit sheets, rigging reports, ANSI E1.47 and manufacturer guides reviewed in the alignment brief and the field checklist.
Design rules
	•	Ask only what applies. Every module opens from a gate question; nothing below a gate shows until the gate is answered.
	•	Three answer states on every question: answered, not applicable, and unable to inspect (with a reason). Reports use the same states, and it keeps skipped items from reading as missed items.
	•	Observe, do not judge outside scope. Rigging components, electrical systems and structure are screened and flagged; the app routes them to a qualified person and does not rate them in detail.
	•	One rating scale everywhere: Immediate attention, Needs attention, Good. Any flagged answer can start a finding.
	•	Photos are tied to questions. A module can require specific photos before it is marked complete.
	•	Earlier answers are reused. Ask a fact once per space; later visits show the stored answer and ask only whether it changed.
The if-then rules below are written so a developer can implement them as conditions on question keys, without needing the full product context.

Action tags sit beside the rating, because rating describes condition and action describes response: out of service, specialist needed, replace, repair, monitor.
Tracking and re-inspection
A finding moves through these statuses, and each change records who made it and when.
	•	Open: created at a site visit.
	•	Triaged: category set and routed to a specialist.
	•	Inspected: a specialist's rating and standard cited are added.
	•	Quoted: a quote or work order references the finding ID.
	•	Scheduled: repair date set.
	•	Repaired: work done, by whom, with before and after photos.
	•	Verified: re-inspection or sign-off confirms the fix.
	•	Closed: feeds the ongoing logs.

Data, build requirements and open questions
Data to store. A district has buildings; a building has spaces; a space has visits; a visit has scopes, answers and photos; findings belong to a space and link back to the answers and photos that raised them. The space record holds the facts that rarely change (stage dimensions, system type, install year, manufacturers) so later visits start pre-filled.
Build requirements (assumptions to confirm)
	•	Works without a connection. Schools in the audit report Wi-Fi dead spots, and stages are often in basements or interior rooms. Sync when a connection returns.
	•	Conditions run on stable question keys, as in the rules above, so the form can change without breaking stored answers.
	•	Dimensions are entered in feet and inches by default.
	•	Photos attach to questions, work with the device camera, and keep the capture time.
	•	Every question supports answered, not applicable, and unable to inspect with a reason.
	•	Reports export per visit and per finding list, in the same field order as the sample rigging reports, so a rigger receives familiar data.
	•	Roles: assessor can create and edit findings; a specialist can add an inspection rating; the owner contact can view and acknowledge; only an assessor or specialist can verify and close.
	•	Reminder timing and alert rules are configurable per district.
