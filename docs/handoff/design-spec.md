## 1. Purpose, users and permissions

### 1.1 What the Design tab is for

The Design tab answers three questions for a sales designer:

1. **"Roughly what would this room cost?"** Quick Design takes a venue type, a size and five
   dimensions, runs fixed sizing equations, and shows a Good / Better / Best budget with a
   generated groundplan. Nothing in it is a real quote until the designer promotes it.
2. **"Where does everything go, and what exactly is on the job?"** The Grid is a plan-sheet
   editor. The designer uploads a plan (or lets the program generate one from a venue template),
   sets its scale, places catalog devices, curtains and wire runs on it, and gets a live bill of
   materials, risers, an equipment schedule and a printable drawing set. A Grid design can become
   a draft quote.
3. **"What does the written specification say?"** The Specs module holds Peak's library of
   specification text and assembles an editable Word specification, one CSI section per file,
   from a bill of materials.

Consulting sits beside these: it tracks paid design engagements (Peak as the specifier), their
phases, internal reviews, milestones and meetings, and produces the consulting proposal.

### 1.2 Users and permissions

- Every screen MUST require a signed-in staff member.
- Quick Design and the designs list accept any signed-in staff member for save, rename, delete,
  review actions and promote.
- The Grid's write actions and the Specs module's write actions MUST require the `create`
  permission. Reading a design needs only a sign-in.
- Grid Settings (Equipment map, wire types, symbol colours, port rules, box types, standard
  general notes) is an admin screen. The Equipment map's allowance confirmation records who
  confirmed it.
- Unit costs MUST NOT reach a Grid client screen. Grid screens receive sell prices only; cost and
  markup are computed and held on the server. Quick Design and the designs list are cost views and
  do show cost and markup. A single offline program has no such boundary; a rebuild that is
  multi-user should keep it.

### 1.3 What a standalone program loses

The app draws on shared records that a standalone program will not have. A rebuild must supply or
replace each of these:

| The Design tab reads | From | Used for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Catalog parts (SKU, description, manufacturer, model, unit, cost, list, category, fabric rates, ports, cable outside diameter, riser tag defaults, designator code) | Catalog | Every placed device, wire, fabric and Equipment-map cell |
| Fixture, System, Hardware and Rack assemblies | Assembly Builder | Equipment-map cells, Auto fill, Quick Design fixture picks, rack groups |
| Device types and the category-to-type map | Catalog → Device types | Library grouping, layers, designator codes, BOM headings |
| Customers, venues, contacts, venue types | CRM | Design header, the title block, intake cover page |
| Customer pricing tier and markup | Estimating Rules / CRM | The price of a Grid quote |
| Estimating Rules: freight %, contingency %, labor %, wire-pull runs, Cable Package rule, sewing %, conduit sizes | Estimating Rules | Totals |
| Quotes | Quotes hub / Estimator | Promote a design; "Design in the Grid" from an estimate |
| Company name, offices, logo | Settings → Branding | Title block, Word letterhead |

## 2. Design records and the intake

### 2.1 Two kinds of design, one list

Every design is a **design record** with id `D-###` (numbering starts at 100, so the first design
is `D-101`). A design record has a layout mode that is set when it is created and MUST never
change:

| Mode | Editor | What the record holds |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `quick` (also any record with no mode) | Quick Design | The whole designer state as one saved `config` |
| `manual` | The Grid | A link to one Grid project (`GRD-####`, numbering starts at 5001) |

There is no conversion between the two. A Grid project cannot exist without a design record.

A design record carries: name; customer name and id; venue (location) id; venue-kind label; size;
tier; width, depth and grid height; owner; the list of systems in scope; a budget in whole
dollars; an optional "incomplete" count (how many lines of the chosen tier still need a part); a
review; a revision list; the id of the draft quote it was promoted to, if any; and the time it was
last updated.

The designs list (`/design/designs`, nav label "The Grid") shows every design with its budget or
"Incomplete — N items need a part", its review state, its customer and its owner, and offers New
design, open, rename, delete, review actions, tasks and Add to Quotes.

### 2.2 Review of a design

A design has a review with a state of none, in review, approved or changes requested; a reviewer;
who submitted it and when; who decided and when; and a note. Actions: submit for review, claim (a
reviewer takes it), approve, request changes (with a note). Reviews of designs appear in the
app's shared Reviews screen beside quote reviews. A design review does not gate anything: an
unreviewed design can still be promoted.

### 2.3 Revisions of a Quick design

A Quick design keeps an append-only list of revisions. Each is a snapshot of the name, tier,
budget, venue, size, dimensions, systems, customer and the full `config`, with a revision number
and a time. Restoring a revision lays its config over the current state through the same cleaning
rules as opening a saved design (section 3.9).

### 2.4 Promote a design to a quote

"Add to Quotes" turns a design into a draft quote. Rules that MUST hold:

- The server re-prices the design at promote time. A price or an "incomplete" flag sent by the
  screen is never used.
- An estimate with any line that needs a part MUST NOT be promoted. The refusal names the count.
- The design is not deleted. It keeps the id of its quote.
- Promoting again while that quote is still a draft updates the same quote. Once the quote has
  left draft, promoting again creates a new quote.
- A Quick design's quote carries: the design's name, customer and venue; value = the server's
  budget; source "sandbox"; a "requote" flag (the budget is not a final price; the estimator
  re-prices it in the Estimator); the customer's pricing tier and markup; and a small spec block
  (venue, size, tier, width, depth, grid, systems, the design id).
- A Grid design promotes through the Grid's own quote builder (chapter 7), never through the
  Quick path.

### 2.5 The Grid intake

Creating a Grid design opens an intake before the drawing workspace. The intake has a cover page
and a choice of **Auto** or **Blank**.

Cover page fields: design title; customer (pick an existing one, or type a new one); venue (pick
one of the customer's venues, type a new one, or skip); contact name; venue / space name;
location / campus name; address; notes; and a "measurement based" tick.

- When a venue is picked, the cover fields pre-fill from it: location = the venue's location name
  (or the customer's name), venue / space = the part of the venue's name after "Location — ", and
  address = street plus "city, state".
- Design name: a typed title always wins; otherwise an untitled design ("Untitled system design")
  takes "Venue — Location" from the cover page (or whichever is filled); otherwise the name is
  unchanged.
- A picked venue that the chosen customer no longer has MUST be refused (a stale form).
- A new customer or venue typed at intake is created in the CRM. The room shape (section 2.8) is
  checked before either is written, so a bad shape never leaves a half-made customer behind.

Venue and size: the same venue list, size presets and dimension fields as Quick Design (section
3.2), plus the template's house fields and movable rooms (sections 2.6–2.7). A new Grid intake
opens as an Auditorium, large, 50 ft wide × 30 ft deep, 20 ft proscenium height, 10 ft wings and a
45 ft grid, with the Auditorium's default systems.

Optional **plan view**: the designer may upload a plan, or pick a plan already on file for the
customer or venue (copied, not shared). It becomes the first sheet. If the upload or copy fails,
the design still opens, with a banner offering a retry.

**Blank** opens the workspace with the generated base sheet (and the plan view, if any) and no
equipment. A Blank design is never Auto filled.

**Auto** shows one card per Grid scope the designer ticks (Rigging, Curtains, Lighting, Audio,
Video). Each card lists the equation items for that scope (chapter 3), priced from the Equipment
map at the card's own tier, with:

- a tier picker per card (Good / Better / Best; default Better);
- an editable quantity per line (the equation's quantity is kept beside it);
- a per-line swap to another catalog part or assembly (the swap prices through the same rules as a
  map cell);
- the line's status: priced from a part, an assembly, an allowance, "Not included", or "needs a
  part" with the reason;
- the card total (equipment only), the count of lines that need a part, the count of allowances,
  and the calculated labor for the scope (shown, not included in the total).

Saving an Auto intake stores the choices per design option and runs Auto fill (chapter 6). If Auto
fill fails, the design still opens with a warning and the choices are kept.

Swap candidates per line:

| Line | Candidates |
| --- | --- |
| Curtain line | Fabric parts with a positive area rate only; shown at a per-square-foot sell; never an assembly |
| Any other line | Catalog parts with a cost or a list price |
| Lighting line | Also Fixture assemblies |
| Rigging line | Also Hardware assemblies |
| Any line | System and Rack assemblies whose own scope equals the card's scope |

### 2.6 Venue kinds and templates

There are seven venues to pick from and six plan kinds behind them. The fields, defaults and
limits are in `design-data.json` → `venueKinds`.

| Venue | Plan kind | Template | Width / depth mean |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Auditorium | proscenium | `proscenium@1` | Proscenium opening; stage depth |
| PAC | proscenium | `proscenium@1` | Proscenium opening; stage depth |
| Church | church | `church-traditional@1` (or `church-contemporary@2`) | Platform width; platform depth |
| Conference | flat | `blackbox@1` plus a platform drawn by code | Room width; room depth |
| Black Box | blackbox | `blackbox@1` | Room width; room depth |
| Gym Stage | gym | `gym-stage@1` | Stage opening; stage depth |
| Arena | arena | `arena@1` | End-stage width; end-stage depth |

A **template** is a background drawing converted once from a CAD file, with hand-written "key
lines" that say how it stretches. All templates share one stretch engine. A template has: a
family (proscenium, church, blackbox or arena) that decides which geometry code draws it; named
regions (the rooms); roles (which region is the stage, the house, the booth, the catwalk);
optional movable rooms; and the size it was drawn at.

Which template a plan draws, in order: the design's own Background override, when it is a template
its plan kind may draw; else the Background of the design's venue type (a CRM setting, admin-only,
Settings → Venue types); else the plan kind's default. The Gym Stage only ever draws a gym
drawing. A template id is versioned (`@1`, `@2`); a redraw ships as a new version and never
changes a sheet already drawn. `church-contemporary@1` is retired: it still draws for designs that
use it but is not offered for new ones.

How each template stretches (plain words; the exact spans are in the `*.keys.ts` files and in
`design-data.json` → `venueTemplates.<id>.stretch`):

- **Auditorium / PAC (`proscenium@1`).** Drawn at a 50 ft opening, 15 ft wings, 30 ft stage, a
  house 79.77 ft wide and 68.84 ft deep. The plaster line never moves. The opening follows
  proscenium width; each wing follows wing width; the stage follows stage depth. Behind the
  stage edge the booth and vestibules keep their size and the rest follows house width, blending
  linearly from the stage's width to the house's width, so the house side walls splay. Front to
  back, the forestage and pit, the catwalk and the cross aisle / back wall / booth row keep their
  size and the two seating bands share the rest of house depth. Walls stay 6 in. The pit is drawn
  only when the Pit Filler system is on. Regions: Stage, Pit, House, Catwalk, Center Aisle, Booth,
  Electrical Room, MISC Rooms.
- **Gym Stage (`gym-stage@1`).** Drawn at a 40 ft opening, 10 ft wings, 20 ft stage, a gym floor
  120 ft × 49.5 ft. Opening, wings and stage depth stretch like the Auditorium. Storage (left of
  the stage) and Electrical Room (right) absorb the difference between the floor width and the
  stage. The Booth (20 ft × 10 ft) keeps its size and is movable. Regions: Stage, Gym Floor,
  Storage, Electrical Room, Booth.
- **Church — Traditional (`church-traditional@1`).** Drawn with a platform 51.39 ft wide and
  26.37 ft deep and a nave 79.97 ft × 55.2 ft. The platform (chancel) follows platform width; the
  side rooms absorb the rest of the nave width; the nave follows nave depth; the apse stays a true
  arc that scales with platform width. Pews are drawn by rule (rows every 3 ft from 6 ft behind
  the platform front to 8 ft short of the back wall, 4 ft clear of the walls, with a 5 ft centre
  aisle). There is no booth; the front-of-house mix position stands in for it. Regions: Platform,
  Apse, Nave, Entry, Choir Room, Electrical Room, Cry Room, Storage.
- **Church — Contemporary (`church-contemporary@2`).** Drawn with a platform 42.66 ft wide at its
  back wall and 23.34 ft deep, and a nave 121.45 ft at its widest and 55.45 ft deep. The nave is
  a diamond-like shape with splayed walls; the pointed platform front stays two true arcs. The
  platform is never deeper than 2.2 × its width. Regions: Platform, Nave, Backstage, four Storage
  rooms, Green Room, Electrical Room, Control Booth, Cry Room.
- **Blackbox (`blackbox@1`).** Drawn 49 ft × 49 ft inside. The room follows width and depth. Four
  rooms outside the walls (Electrical Room, Booth, two Storage) keep their size and are movable.
  The Conference venue uses the same drawing and adds a low platform across the front.
- **Arena (`arena@1`).** Drawn with a floor 90 ft × 134 ft, 13 ft round corners and a 15 ft
  seating bowl. The court keeps its share of the floor (300/540 of the width, 564/804 of the
  length). The Booth and Electrical Room move along the bowl's straight sides. The end stage is a
  rectangle drawn by code from stage width and stage depth, movable along the floor's straight
  edges, and shrunk to fit when it is too big for the edge it sits on.

House fields. Templates with a house add two fields (three for the arena) after the main
dimensions. Their limits depend on the other fields:

| Template | Field | Minimum | Maximum | Default |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `proscenium@1` | House width | the larger of 45 and the opening | 200 | opening + 2 × wing |
| | House depth | 40 | 200 | 68.84 |
| `gym-stage@1` | Gym floor width | opening + 2 × wing + 9 | 250 | the larger of 120 and the minimum |
| | Gym floor depth | 21 | 200 | 49.5 |
| `church-traditional@1` | Nave width | platform width + 16 | 200 | the larger of 79.97 and the minimum |
| | Nave depth | 20 | 200 | 55.2 |
| `church-contemporary@2` | Nave width | platform width + 24 | 250 | the larger of 121.45 and the minimum |
| | Nave depth | 20 | 200 | 55.45 |
| `arena@1` | Floor width | 59 | 250 | 90 |
| | Floor length | 88 | 400 | 134 |
| | Bowl depth | 0 | 60 | 15 |

A blank house field means "use the default". A typed value outside the limits is clamped, and the
plan shows a one-line warning when the clamp changed what was asked for (for example "The house
is narrower than the stage, so its side walls slant inward."). The exact sentences are in
`design-data.json`.

### 2.7 Movable rooms

Some rooms can be moved along walls: the Gym Stage Booth (back, left or right wall of the gym
floor), the four Blackbox rooms (any of the four walls), and the Arena's Booth, Electrical Room
(the bowl's four straight sides) and end stage (the floor's four straight edges).

- A position is a wall id plus a number from 0 to 1 along that wall's usable run. No position
  means "home" (as drawn).
- A moved room keeps its drawn size and turns to face in.
- Two rooms on one wall keep 24 in between them. When they cannot all fit, the plan warns "Not
  everything fits on the … wall".
- In Quick Design the designer drags a room's handle; in the intake there are fields. The server
  cleans every position: unknown rooms and walls are dropped and the number is clamped.
- The front-of-house mix position follows the Booth wherever it sits.
- When a base sheet is generated, the positions are stamped on the design. Later edits to the
  intake MUST NOT move rooms on a sheet that has already been drawn.

### 2.8 Room shape editor (slice 1)

The intake has an optional "Shape the room" step.

- Pressing it **freezes** the stretched template into a room shape: a list of rooms and zones in
  feet, each with a closed outline of 3 to 64 points and one edge per point. The origin is the
  template's centreline at its fixed line (the plaster line or the platform front); x runs right
  and y runs down the sheet.
- An edge is a straight line, a true arc (stored as a bulge: the tangent of a quarter of its
  sweep) or a free-form cubic curve with two handles. An edge is a wall unless it is marked open
  (an opening, or a stage lip). Walls versus openings are read from the template's double lines.
- A room is walled; a zone has a dashed outline and no walls. A room may carry roles: stage,
  house, booth, catwalk. Each role sits on at most one room.
- Editing: drag a corner (with snapping, and "glue" so a corner shared by two rooms within half an
  inch moves both); Add point; make an edge a Curve or Free-form; switch an edge between Wall and
  Opening; type a length, a radius or an arc length; undo and redo.
- Once a shape exists, the intake's dimension fields are locked until **Reset to template**.
- Limits: at most 40 rooms; coordinates within ±2,000 ft; bulge within ±10; names up to 60
  characters. The server MUST re-check the whole shape and refuse a bad one.
- A base sheet generated from a shape is drawn from the shape, gets one Space per room (each
  remembers the room it came from), gets an exact scale from the shape's size, and stamps the
  shape and its frame (where the sheet sits in feet) on the design. Auto fill reads that stamp,
  never the shape still being edited.
- The fixed room-type list (18 types, with a default drop size each) exists for slice 2 and is in
  `src/lib/design/room-shape/types.ts`. Slices 2 (add rooms and zones, more roles) and 3 (reopen
  after Create, DXF) are not built.

### 2.9 The generated base sheet

Every Grid design gets a generated base sheet unless a real plan replaces it:

- It is the venue plan as Quick Design draws it (section 3.4), rendered as a picture and stored as
  the design's first sheet.
- It is calibrated automatically from the plan's own scale.
- It gets **starter Spaces**: one room polygon per template region, in the template's order
  (Stage, Pit, House, Catwalk, … for the Auditorium). A plan with no template gets three: Audience
  view, Stage and FOH / control, at fixed fractions of the sheet.
- The template id and the movable rooms' positions are stamped on the design.
- A design whose base sheet was drawn before templates existed keeps the old schematic's layout
  for Auto fill, so a re-fill lands on the plan the design actually has.
- The base sheet is never cropped or rotated.

## 3. Quick Design

### 3.1 The screen

Quick Design is one screen with a configuration panel and five views of the same design:
**Estimate**, **BOM**, **Plan**, **Schedule** and **Control riser**. The designer picks a venue
and a size, adjusts dimensions, switches systems on and off, picks a tier and reads the result.
Nothing is saved until Save; Save revision adds a snapshot; Add to Quotes promotes (section 2.4).

### 3.2 Inputs

| Input | Values | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Venue | Auditorium, Church, PAC, Conference, Black Box, Gym Stage, Arena | Sets the plan kind and default systems |
| Size | Small, Medium, Large | Multiplies the venue's base dimensions by 0.78, 1.00 or 1.28, and separately drives the equations |
| Width | 20–80 ft | |
| Depth | 14–52 ft | |
| Grid (or ceiling) height | 16–84 ft | |
| Wing width | 4–40 ft | Shown for Auditorium, PAC and Gym Stage only |
| Proscenium height | 10–50 ft | Shown for Auditorium, PAC and Gym Stage only |
| House fields | per template | Section 2.6 |
| Systems | eight on/off switches | Rigging, Curtains, Fixtures (lighting), Controls, Acoustical Shell, Pit Filler, Audio, Video |
| Tier | Good, Better, Best | Default Better |
| Contingency | 0–25 %, whole numbers | New designs start at the Estimating Rules default (10) |

Picking a venue or a size sets each dimension to `clamp(round(base × multiplier), min, max)`.
After that the designer may type any value inside the limits; the size key stays as picked. A
field a venue does not show keeps its value and is still read by the equations.

Venue base dimensions (width, depth, grid, wing, proscenium height) and default systems:

| Venue | W | D | G | Wing | PH | Systems on by default |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Auditorium | 36 | 26 | 24 | 12 | 18 | Rigging, Curtains, Fixtures, Audio, Acoustical |
| Church | 34 | 22 | 24 | 0 | 18 | Curtains, Fixtures, Audio, Video |
| PAC | 48 | 34 | 58 | 18 | 28 | All eight |
| Conference | 50 | 30 | 34 | 0 | 24 | Rigging, Curtains, Fixtures, Controls, Audio, Video |
| Black Box | 26 | 24 | 18 | 0 | 16 | Rigging, Curtains, Fixtures, Controls, Audio, Acoustical |
| Gym Stage | 40 | 30 | 26 | 10 | 14 | Rigging, Curtains, Fixtures, Audio |
| Arena | 40 | 16 | 60 | 0 | 20 | Rigging, Fixtures, Controls, Audio, Video |

These are base values. A wing of 0 is lifted to the 4 ft minimum by the clamp when a venue or size

Sub-options per system:

| System | Choice | Options |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Rigging | one | Counterweight, Dead Hung, Motorized |
| Curtains | any | Draw, Legs, Border, Scenery (track), Full (full-stage traveller) |

### 3.3 The estimate equations

The equations produce **item keys and quantities only**. They contain no prices. Pricing is a
separate step (chapter 4). Every item has a stable key `system:itemKey` and takes its name and
unit from the vocabulary (section 4.1).

Helpers:

- `pick(a, b, c)` = `a` for size Small, `b` for Medium, `c` for Large.
- `dBlk` = `floor(D / 10)` (10-ft depth blocks).
- `sets` = `pick(5, 6, 7) × dBlk`.
- `pipeLen` = `W + 4` when the venue's plan kind is proscenium (2 ft of batten overhang each
  side), otherwise `W`. The Gym Stage's plan kind is "gym", so it uses `W`.
- `electrics` = `clamp(round(D / 12), 2, 5)` when Fixtures is on, else 0. `E` = `max(1, electrics)`.
- `wUnit` = `max(1, round(W / 8))`.

Display statistics (not priced): `lineSets = clamp(round(D × 1.5), 12, 72)`;
`drapeArea = round(W × G)`; `dimmerRacks = max(1, ceil(conventional fixtures / 48))` when Fixtures
is on, where conventional fixtures are every fixture line except Mover.

**Rigging — Counterweight**

| Item | Quantity |
| --- | --- |
| Headblock, Footblock, Arbor, T-bar track, Lock rail | `sets` each |
| Handline | `sets × 2 × G` ft |
| Pipe | `sets × pipeLen` ft |
| Loftblock | `loft` = `sets × max(1, floor(W / 10))` |
| Suspension Method (aircraft cable) | `loft × (2G + W)` ft |
| Beginning Termination | `loft × 2` |
| Batten Termination (chain wrap) | `loft` |

**Rigging — Dead Hung**

| Item | Quantity |
| --- | --- |
| Rigging point | `points` = `sets × floor(W / 10)` |
| Pipe | `sets × pipeLen` ft |

### 3.4 The plan view and venue geometry

The Plan view draws a generated groundplan on a canvas 640 units wide, stage or platform at the
top. One shared geometry function per family produces everything that needs to agree: the plan
drawing, the Grid's generated base sheet, the wall-drag handles, the starter Spaces and Auto
fill's layout frame. For the proscenium family this function is `prosGeom`: it stretches the
template to the design's dimensions (section 2.6), lays it on the canvas with fixed margins, and
returns the canvas size, the scale (canvas units per foot), each region's outline, and the boxes
Auto fill uses (the stage, the house, the booth, the catwalk) plus the stage-edge curve.
`churchGeom`, `blackboxGeom` and `arenaGeom` do the same for their families. The arena's also
returns the end stage's centre, size and the angle it is turned to.

The plan then draws the systems that are on, by rule, as symbols: rigging set lines and points,
curtain lines, electrics with fixtures, loudspeakers and line arrays, the console mark at the
front-of-house mix position, projection screens, and a legend that lists only the symbols
actually drawn for that venue kind. Equipment symbols are drawn at half their nominal size and
plan text at 85 % of its nominal size. Dimension chains show the main dimensions.

Interaction on the plan:

- Wall handles: dragging a house or nave wall changes the house width or depth (within the limits
  in section 2.6). Pre-template saves stored a dragged half-width for the proscenium house; it is
  still read (house width = 2 × half-width) when no house width is saved.
- Movable-room handles (section 2.7).
- Reset puts the house back to its default size and every movable room back home. On a plan with
  rooms but no wall handles (Blackbox, Arena) Reset moves the rooms only.

Proscenium doors and the hand-placed "manual layout" mode of the original prototype are gone. A
saved design may still carry `mode: "manual"` and a `placements` list; they are kept as saved and
not drawn.

### 3.5 Cable Package and wire pull

Two lines are added after the equations, at the tier being priced:

- **Cable Package** (Fixtures only): `qty = ceil(fixtures × per fixture × tier multiplier)`,
  where `fixtures` is the total quantity of the Par, Front, Cyc, Side light and Automated lines
  **as the designer has them** (a typed quantity wins over the equation's). The product is rounded
  to the thousandth before `ceil`, so floating-point noise never buys an extra package. Defaults:
  1 per fixture; multipliers 1.00 / 1.15 / 1.30 for Good / Better / Best. No fixtures, or 0 per
  fixture, or Fixtures off: no line. The line shows its own math, for example
  "24 fixtures × 1 × 1.15".
- **Wire pull** (Rigging, Lighting, Controls, Audio, Video; one line per system that is on):
  `feet = ceil((stage width + stage depth + house depth) × runs × tier multiplier)`. `runs`
  defaults to 0, which emits no line. No design form collects a house depth, so it always counts
  0 and the line says "House depth isn't in the design — not counted". A blank stage dimension
  counts 0 with a note.

Both rules are Estimating Rules. Limits: per fixture 0–10; runs 0–20; any tier multiplier 0–3.

### 3.6 The line-set dial

Each tier has a line-set dial (1–300, whole number). Blank means the equation's own count
(`rigSets`). When the dial differs, every rigging quantity for that tier becomes
`max(0, round(quantity × dial ÷ rigSets))`. The dial rescales quantities only.

A saved design stores the three dial values it was priced with, so it prices the same on every
machine. Only a brand-new, unsaved design follows the dials remembered in the designer's browser.

### 3.7 Quantity overrides

On the BOM view the designer may type a quantity for any line, per tier. The value is a whole
number of 0 or more (anything else becomes 0). Overrides are keyed by system and by the line's
**name**, so a relabelled item reads its old override through an alias table (in
`design-data.json` → `equipmentVocab.relabelAliases`). The Scenery track override is in feet; a
design saved before that change stored a count of runs, which is dropped on open (there is no
safe conversion) and the calculated feet are used.

Overrides are applied after pricing: they change quantities and therefore totals, never unit
prices.

### 3.8 Totals

For the selected tier, over the systems that are on:

1. Material = Σ quantity × unit sell.
2. Install = `round(Σ system material × labor % × labor tier multiplier)`. Labor % is per system
   (default 18 for every system; multipliers 1.00 / 1.15 / 1.30). The Estimate view lists each
   system's labor; the total rounds once.
3. Freight = `round(material × freight %)`. Default 5 %.
4. Contingency = `round((material + install + freight) × contingency %)`.
5. Grand total = material + install + freight + contingency, shown in whole dollars.

The view also shows material cost and markup, `(material sell − material cost) ÷ material cost`.

The tier table also carries a cost multiplier and a price multiplier per tier (0.80 / 0.84 for
Good, 1 / 1 for Better, 1.30 / 1.42 for Best). They are inert: every system priced from the
Equipment map ignores them. A rebuild should not apply them.

The Estimate view shows all three tiers side by side with their totals and, per tier, an editable
description per system (the "tier definitions": a blurb, a spec sentence per system, a line-set
count and a default fabric per curtain type). Tier definitions are remembered per browser, not per
design, except the line-set counts (section 3.6). Shipped texts are in `design-data.json` →
`tiers`.

**Incomplete.** If any line with quantity above 0 on a system that is on has no price, the tier
shows "Incomplete — N items need a part" instead of a dollar total, and Add to Quotes is refused.
A design whose saved state cannot be computed counts as one item needing a part and $0.

### 3.9 Save, open and clean

Save writes the display fields (name, venue label, size, tier, dimensions, systems, budget,
incomplete count) and the whole state as `config`, with the three line-set dial values and a
marker that the Scenery-track override is in feet. The auto-name suffix is
"<Tier> design (W'×D')".

The server computes the budget and the incomplete count itself on every save. Opening a saved
design applies the same cleaning the server uses, so the screen shows what the server prices:

- Dimensions clamped to their limits (a non-number becomes the middle of the range).
- House fields clamped to the template's limits; a non-positive value becomes "default".
- Contingency truncated to a whole number and clamped to 0–25.
- An unknown tier becomes Better.
- Quantity overrides: whole numbers of 0 or more; anything else dropped; aliases applied.
- Movable-room positions cleaned (section 2.7).

A seed design with no `config` is rebuilt from its display fields.

### 3.10 Schedule and Control riser views

- **Schedule** lists the line sets implied by the design (it uses the Lineset Builder's rules,
  which stay in the app).
- **Control riser** is a fixed one-line block diagram built from what is in scope: 480 V building
  service; main disconnect and line filter; then, when present, the lighting and automation
  console (Controls with Console), the architectural control processor (Architectural), the show
  network switch (Data), rigging drive racks × `max(1, ceil(lineSets / 8))` (Motorized rigging),
  dimmer / relay racks × `dimmerRacks` (Fixtures), the amplifier / DSP rack (Audio), and video
  processing and switching (Video). An E-stop is shown when Controls with Console is on. With
  none of those in scope the view is empty.

## 4. Equipment map and pricing

### 4.1 The vocabulary

The equations can emit a fixed set of items, the **equipment vocabulary**. There are 51 rows: 46
equation items plus one "Wire pull" row for each of the five wire systems. Each row has:

- a stable key `system:itemKey` (for example `rigging:headblock`); keys never change, so a relabel
  never orphans a mapping;
- a label and a unit (`ea`, `ft`, `lot`, `sqft`); the label is the one name used by Quick Design,
  the Auto cards and the Equipment map;
- how Auto places it on a plan: `each` (one marker per unit), `lot` (one marker carrying the
  quantity), `curtain` (a curtain drop-in on the mapped fabric), or `none` (never placed);
- search words that seed the map's suggested catalog matches.

### 4.2 The Equipment map

The Equipment map (Grid Settings → Equipment map, admin only) says, for every vocabulary row and
every tier, what prices that item. It starts **empty**. There is no built-in dollar anywhere in the
estimate: an unmapped row is "needs a part" and never prices.

A cell (one row × one tier) is one of:

| Cell | Meaning | Unit cost | Unit sell |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Part | A catalog SKU | The part's cost | The part's list price, else cost × (1 + catalog markup) |
| Assembly | A Fixture, System, Hardware or Rack assembly | The assembly's included cost | The assembly's included sell, else cost × (1 + catalog markup) |
| Allowance | A confirmed dollar amount for a product with no catalog row | The amount | Amount × (1 + catalog markup) |
| Not included | This tier has no solution for the item | $0 | $0 |
| Empty | Not mapped yet | — | — (needs a part) |

Rules:

- A row may be marked "Same for all tiers": the Good cell is the one that counts and is copied to
  Better and Best.
- An allowance MUST be confirmed with a tick ("I confirm this allowance"); an unconfirmed one is
  never used. The amount must be above $0 and at most $10,000,000. It may carry an internal note
  (why) and a customer-facing description, each up to 200 characters. Who confirmed it and when
  are recorded, and are re-stamped only when the amount or the note changes.
- "Not included" is a resolved state: it is never "Incomplete", prices $0, places nothing and
  puts no line on a quote.
- A curtain row maps to a **fabric part** only (category Fabric, or a soft-goods part sold per
  square foot). It cannot map to an assembly. A fabric part with no usable rate is "needs a
  part" ("No $/sq ft set").
- A cell that points at something that no longer prices is "needs a part" with the reason: the
  part is no longer in the catalog; the part has no price; the assembly was deleted; the assembly
  has no priced parts.
- Row status shown in the editor: **Mapped** (every tier is a part, an assembly or Not included),
  **Allowance** (any tier is an allowance), **Needs a part** (any tier is empty or does not
  price). Status is computed from resolved prices, not from what is stored.
- The editor warns (advisory only) when a mapped part's unit differs from the row's unit: the
  equation's quantity is in the row's unit, so a per-foot row priced by a per-each part
  misprices.
- Two admins editing different rows MUST NOT overwrite each other: each row is saved on its own.
- The catalog markup is one setting (the catalog's default markup, 43 % as shipped). It is a
  rate, never a dollar; an unusable value falls back to 43 %.

### 4.3 Pricing a line

1. Take the line's cell for the tier being priced, or the per-design swap for that row (a Quick
   Design fixture pick, or an Auto card swap), which wins.
2. No cell, or a cell that does not price: cost $0, sell $0, status "needs a part".
3. "Not included": cost $0, sell $0.
4. A curtain line priced by a fabric part: the cost of one drape is

   `round(finished width × (1 + fullness %) × finished height × fabric $/sq ft × (1 + sewing %) × panels)`

   and its sell is that cost × (1 + catalog markup). The fabric rate is, in order: the part's own
   curtain area rate; the seed rate for that SKU; the part's cost per square foot; the part's cost
   when its unit is square feet; else none. Sewing % is one Estimating Rule (default 10, 0–100). A
   confirmed allowance on a curtain row is already a per-drape cost and skips this math.
5. Any other line: the cell's unit cost and unit sell.
6. Line total = quantity × unit sell.

Seed fabric rates ($ per sq ft of sewn fabric, fabric only): `RB-CHAR-25` 3.64, `RB-EN-22` 2.84,
`RB-EN-16` 2.10, `RB-MV-MN` 4.37, `RB-MUS` 0.90. The code marks `RB-MV-MN` as a placeholder.

### 4.4 The "Incomplete" rule

An estimate is **Incomplete** when any line with quantity above 0, on a system in scope, has
status "needs a part". Then:

- Quick Design and the designs list show "Incomplete — N items need a part" instead of a dollar
  total.
- Add to Quotes MUST refuse. The server re-checks on every promote path (Quick Design, the designs
  list, Home), including designs saved before this rule existed.
- A needs-a-part line is counted and never summed.
- On the Grid, Auto never places a needs-a-part line. Because the plan then lacks that equipment,
  the Grid's quote also refuses while an option's Auto choices have such a line in a scope that
  was given a tier (a line edited to quantity 0 was dropped on purpose and does not count).

### 4.5 Scope targets on the Grid

The Grid's Scope panel shows, for each of the five Grid scopes in scope, a **target**: what the
equations say that scope should cost at a tier, beside the value actually placed. Targets are
computed on the server and sent as sell numbers only. For a design made with Auto, a scope's
target is its Auto card (the chosen tier, swaps and quantity edits). For other scopes the panel
treats tier as a lens: it holds Good, Better and Best targets and the designer switches between
them. The Scope panel's inputs (venue, size, dimensions, systems and sub-options) stay editable
for the life of the design; targets are always computed fresh.

### 4.6 Virtual parts

Auto can place an assembly or an allowance on a plan. They are carried as virtual part ids:
`asm:<assembly id>` and `allow:<row key>:<tier>`. A virtual part prices live each time (it is
never frozen on the placement). A virtual part that no longer prices (assembly deleted, assembly
with no priced member, allowance no longer confirmed) shows on the plan as "needs a part —
replace or re-fill" and MUST refuse the quote by name; it never prices at $0.

## 5. The Grid workspace

### 5.1 Layout

`/design/grid/<id>` is a full-window docked workspace:

- a toolbar of icon buttons across the top;
- a **left pane**: Property Editor (the selected device or devices), System Status (a list of
  warnings with fixes: calibrate, map, upload), and "Placed vs. target" (the Scope panel);
- the **plan canvas** in the middle, with sheet tabs and a Plan / Spreadsheet toggle;
- a **right pane**: Browser (a tree of sheets, spaces and devices), Layers, Spaces, Wires, BOM and
  Revisions;
- a bottom **Product Library** of numbered symbol tiles;
- a status bar.

Side and bottom panes resize and collapse. Sizes and collapse state are remembered per viewer in
the browser. Defaults and limits in pixels: left 280 (200–520), right 280 (200–520), bottom 220
(120–480). The side panes start collapsed in a window narrower than 1,100 px; the Library never
does.

### 5.2 Sheets

A design has an ordered list of sheets. A sheet is a PDF or an image (PNG, JPEG, WebP, GIF).

Upload:

- Up to 25 MB per file. The server MUST check what the file really is from its first bytes and
  refuse anything that is not a PDF or one of the four image types (including markup and SVG
  dressed up as one).
- A multi-page PDF becomes **one sheet per page**, named "<file name> — p.<n>", up to 60 pages.
  A PDF with more pages, or whose split pages would total more than 100 MB, is kept as one sheet
  with a note.
- Uploading a real plan to a design whose generated base sheet has nothing drawn on it removes
  the generated sheet. If the generated sheet has content, it is kept and the designer is told
  why. Before removing it the design saves an automatic revision, "Auto-saved before removing the
  generated plan".

**Adjust sheet** (crop and rotate). Every new sheet opens in Adjust sheet; later it is on the
sheet tab's menu as "Crop & rotate…". With several new sheets it walks them ("Sheet 2 of 5",
"Skip the rest").

- Per page: a crop rectangle and quarter turns. The crop is at least 2 % of the page on each
  side.
- Adjusting makes a **new** sheet derived from the original upload; it never edits the original
  and never chains (a re-adjust derives from the original again).
- A page MUST NOT be adjusted while anything sits on it (devices, spaces, wires or a scale). The
  dialog says what is on the page. Because of that rule every reference can simply move to the
  new sheet: sheet order (same position), placements, spaces, wires, calibrations, the sheet's
  default level and the drawing set's exclusions.
- Revisions are history and are not rewritten.
- The generated base sheet is never adjusted.

Removing a sheet removes it from the list. A write aimed at a sheet the design no longer lists is
refused with "That sheet is no longer on this design — reload and try again."

### 5.3 Calibration

Each page of each sheet has at most one calibration. The designer draws a line of known length
and types the length and unit (ft, in, m or mm).

- The stored scale is real units per one full page width. Distances are measured with y scaled by
  the page's height ÷ width, so a measurement is the same at any zoom.
- Feet display as feet and inches (12'-6"); other units as decimals.
- Wire length, snap-to-grid in real units, the printed scale and conduit lengths all need the
  page's calibration. Recalibrating reprices every wire on the page at once; nothing is cached.
- Clearing a calibration leaves wires "unmeasured" (section 7.1), never guessed.

### 5.4 Tools

One tool is active at a time, in this priority when several modes are set: Calibrate, Space, Wire,
Curtain, Place, Pan, Select.

| Tool | Key | What it does |

| Select | V | Click a device, shift-click to add or remove, drag a marquee (shift = add to the selection) |
| Place | P | Click the plan to drop the armed part from the Library |
| Curtain | — | Click to drop a curtain of the armed type, then fill in its dialog |

### 5.5 Placements

A placement is one marker on one page of one sheet, at a position stored as fractions (0–1) of the
page. It names a part by id. It may also carry:

| Field | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Category | A free-text label of up to 40 characters, set by the designer; independent of scope |
| Designator | Section 5.13 |
| Riser tag overrides | Section 8.3 |
| Space pin | The room this device is pinned to, whatever its position |
| Group | The id of the device it sits inside (section 5.8) |
| Rack link | The rack assembly this holder is linked to (section 5.8) |
| Curtain | A curtain's spec; makes this placement a curtain (section 5.6) |
| Quantity | A lot: one marker standing for N units (1 to 100,000) |
| Auto tag | Set by Auto fill: scope, row and tier (chapter 6) |
| Option | Which design option it belongs to (section 5.15) |

Every placement has exactly one **scope**, computed from its part each time: the part's own Grid
scope if set, else its catalog group (Lighting Controls and Fixtures → Lighting; Video Controls →
Video; Speakers and Audio Controls → Audio; Curtains → Curtains), else its trade (Lighting →
Lighting; Rigging → Rigging), else Unscoped. An ungrouped AV part is Unscoped on purpose: the trade
alone cannot say Audio or Video. A curtain's scope is Curtains.

A placement whose part has left the catalog MUST stay visible, flagged, at $0. It is never
dropped silently.

### 5.6 Curtains

A curtain is a priced line of its own, not a catalog unit. It has a type (Border, Draw, Full or
Leg), a name, a finished width and height in feet, a fullness (Flat 0 %, 50 %, 75 % or 100 %) and
a fabric (a catalog fabric part; the placement's part id is that fabric).

- Each curtain is its own BOM line; curtains are never grouped, even with the same fabric.
- Price: sewn area × the fabric's rate × (1 + sewing %) is the cost; the sell applies the
  customer's tier markup on a quote. The editor shows sell prices only.
- A curtain never gets a designator, never holds devices and is never inside one.
- A curtain may carry a spec key (section 10.5); when none is typed, one is derived from its name
  and type.

### 5.7 Spaces

A Space is a named room polygon on one page (3 or more corners), with a colour from a six-colour
cycle and, optionally, a riser level.

- Which devices belong to a space is **computed**: a device belongs to the smallest space that
  contains it. Redrawing a space reassigns devices at once.
- One exception: a device may be **pinned** to a space on the same sheet and page. A pin wins over
  position. A pin to a deleted space, or to a space on another page, is ignored; deleting a space
  clears pins to it.
- Spaces belong to the design, not to an option.
- The BOM can be rolled up by space (one row per space with devices, in the design's space order,
  then "Unassigned"), with each space's total split by scope or by category.

### 5.8 Groups and racks

Any device can hold others (a rack, a booth, a panel). A member names its holder.

- One level only: a holder cannot be a member, and a member cannot hold.
- A holder and its members MUST be on the same option, sheet and page.
- At most 200 members per holder.
- A member has no plan position of its own: its sheet, page, position and space pin mirror the
  holder's. The plan does not draw it; the schedule lists it under its holder.
- Taking devices out puts them in a row beside the holder, wrapping every 8.
- Deleting a holder asks whether its members are deleted too or kept.

A placed **rack assembly** (`asm:<rack id>`) shows the rack's devices as its group, read-only,
with "Open rack →". They come from the rack record each time and are never stored as placements,
so nothing is priced twice. "Link to rack…" turns a plain holder into that rack (refused while it
holds devices of its own); "Unlink" puts the old part back and copies the rack's devices in as
ordinary members, each priced on its own from then on. Swapping the holder's part drops the link.

### 5.9 Wires

A wire run is a polyline of 2 or more waypoints on one page, carrying a per-length catalog part (a
part whose unit prices by length).

- Its length is the polyline's length in page widths × the page's scale. The page's height ÷
  width is stored on the wire when it is drawn, so the length can be recomputed anywhere.
- When both ends snap onto placed devices, the wire remembers them. If both devices have ports,
  the wire is checked: there must be a pair of ports that can connect (compatible direction, and
  a wire type that carries both ports' connection types), and the connection type is stored.
- A wire on an uncalibrated page is **unmeasured**: it is counted and shown, never priced by a
  guess.
- A **RiserLink** is a typed-length cable run between two things that are not on one calibrated
  page (section 8.2). It prices exactly like a wire.

### 5.10 Layers

Three kinds of layer share one hidden set, and they combine with AND:

- **Scope** (Lighting, Rigging, Curtains, Audio, Video, Unscoped): hiding a scope hides everything
  in it.
- **Device type**, within its scope: hiding a type under Audio does not hide it under Lighting.
- **Category** (the designer's free-text labels): hiding a category hides that label across every
  scope. A device with no category is never affected by a category toggle.

Scope colours: Lighting `#e08b1f`, Rigging `#6b4fa1`, Curtains `#b0367c`, Audio `#2e9e6b`, Video
`#3155a8`, Unscoped `#8c919c`.

### 5.11 Multi-select, align, clipboard, snap and undo

- **Bulk actions** on a selection: Set category, Replace part, Delete, Set space, Put inside /
  take out, Set riser tag fields, Set designators. Each batch is all-or-nothing and is one write.
  A batch is at most 2,000 items.
- **Align**: left, centre, right, top, middle, bottom, to the extremes of the selection (needs 2
  or more). **Distribute**: evenly along x or y between the two end items, which stay put (needs
  3 or more).
- **Copy / Cut / Paste / Duplicate**: a copy is a snapshot relative to the selection's top-left
  corner. A paste lands offset by 1.5 % of the sheet. A pasted device is an ordinary hand
  placement: Auto tags and other provenance are never copied. Group links inside the selection
  are kept.
- **Snap to grid** is off by default. On a calibrated page the spacing is 6 in, 1 ft, 2 ft or
  5 ft; a spacing that would be finer than 0.1 % or coarser than 50 % of the sheet is not offered.
  On an uncalibrated page the step is 1 % of the sheet. Cells are square on the sheet.
- **Undo / Redo**: 100 steps, in the browser, for the page session. A new edit clears redo. Undo
  covers moves, deletes (restored with everything attached), category, part, designator, riser
  tag, space pin and group changes. Undoing a delete restores the same records; redoing it
  deletes them again.

### 5.12 Symbol display and object symbols

Each design saves a symbol **size** (25 % to 400 %, in 5 % steps; default 100 %) and a **mode**:
Generic or Object.

- The size scales markers, their click and snap targets, the selection ring and the drawing set's
  plan sheets. It never scales the riser. A marker's base size is 44 × 30 unless the part gives
  its own. The click radius scales too, never below half its base, so small markers stay
  grabbable.
- **Generic**: a marker is the device type's icon in a tile coloured by its symbol colour key.
- **Object**: a marker is a product drawing. It resolves part's own drawing → the device type's
  drawing → the generic symbol. On the riser it resolves part's riser drawing → the plan drawing
  → generic.
- Drawings are part documents of two kinds, `symbol` and `riser`. SVG is accepted for these only,
  MUST be sanitised, and MUST only ever be drawn as an image (never inlined into the page).
- Object drawings show on the plan, the drawing set, the Library tiles and the riser.

### 5.13 Designators

Every placed device has a designator: its code and a number (`MIC-1`).

- Numbering is per code within one design option. A new device takes the lowest free number. A
  lot of N takes N consecutive numbers, stores the first and shows the range (`LX-1–24`).
- Numbers print padded to two digits by default (`CRO-01`); an app setting switches to one.
- The code is, in order: the part's own designator code; its device type's code; for a part not
  mapped to a type, the type of the placement's category, then of the part's category; else the
  system letter of its scope (L, A, V, R for Rigging and Curtains, G).
- Gaps are kept until **Renumber…**, which re-issues numbers for all devices, one code or the
  selection, in reading order. **Apply current type codes** re-issues each device in its current
  type code.
- Reading order: sheet order; page; space (in the design's space order, devices in no space
  last); rows 2 % of the sheet tall, top to bottom; then left to right.
- Anything not shaped `<code>-<number>` is a custom designator: shown as typed, never renumbered,
  never counted. Up to 24 characters; control and invisible characters are stripped; "MIC - 1"
  becomes "MIC-1".
- Duplicates (overlapping numbers in one code, or equal custom text) are drawn amber and are not
  blocked.
- Replace part: a number issued in the old type's code is re-issued when the code changes; a
  custom or hand-renamed designator is kept.
- Curtains never take a designator.
- A design made before designators existed is numbered once when its editor is opened.

Designators show on the plan, the Property Editor, the Browser, the Spreadsheet's Devices tab
(inline edit, filters, sort, undo), the schedules and the drawing set. They are not on the BOM,
quotes or customer documents.

### 5.14 Product Library, favorites and device types

The Library lists parts as numbered symbol tiles under: Favorites, Recent, each scope → device
type, Assemblies and Curtains. It has a search box (description, model, maker, model number and
former SKUs) and a manufacturer filter. A viewer keeps up to 300 favorites and 40 recents.

Device types group the catalog for the Grid. There are 25 shipped types (in `design-data.json` →
`deviceTypes`), each with a scope, an icon and a designator code. A catalog category maps to at
most one type; confident matches are applied automatically and the rest wait for an admin. Device
types and the mapping are managed in the Catalog and stay in the app.

A design opened from an estimate has an extra **From estimate** tray (section 5.16).

### 5.15 Options and revisions

An **option** is a variant of one design: its own devices, wires, BOM, custom items, accessories,
labor overrides, risers and quote, on the design's shared sheets, scales and spaces. Good, Better
and Best can be three options; a hand-made design has one, named "Design". Options can be added
(a copy of another), renamed and removed.

A **revision** is an append-only snapshot of the design's changeable state: name, sheet list,
devices, scales, spaces, wires, options, risers, Auto choices and levels. A revision is cut by
hand ("Save revision", with a note), automatically when a quote is created or updated from the
design, and as bookkeeping when an older revision is restored. Restore is non-destructive: it
makes the old state current and records a new revision. Sheets are referenced, not copied; a
restore never removes a sheet added since, and brings a sheet back when the restored devices,
spaces or wires need it.

### 5.16 Estimate-owned designs

From an estimate's Build package step, "Design in the Grid →" creates (or opens) one Grid design
linked to that estimate. It applies to quotes built in the Estimator; flame-test, repair,
inspection, consulting and rental quotes do not qualify.

- One Grid design per estimate. Its base option is **estimate-owned**: the estimate owns parts and
  prices; the option is drawings only.
- Every action that would write from the Grid to a quote MUST refuse for that design, and the Grid
  shows "Open estimate →" in place of its quote button.
- The intake is prefilled from the estimate and is Blank only.
- The Library shows a **From estimate** tray: each part on the estimate with "placed / needed",
  arm-to-place, and alternates and options collapsed.
- The conduit riser's price switches are ignored for an estimate-owned option (section 8.4).
- A copy of an estimate-owned option is an ordinary option with no quote.

## 6. Auto fill

### 6.1 When it runs and what it replaces

Auto fill turns an option's Auto choices (section 2.5) into placements on the **generated base
sheet**. It runs only from a user action: the intake's first save, and "Change equipment…" on one
scope (which re-fills that scope). It never runs on page load.

- It re-prices the cards from the Equipment map and the live catalog first.
- It replaces the option's **untouched** Auto devices in the chosen scopes. A device the designer
  moved or edited has lost its Auto tag and is kept.
- Kept devices still count: a device that lost its tag by a hand edit remembers the scope and row
  it was painted for, and a re-fill subtracts those units from the row's new quantity. Only the
  difference is placed, never below zero.
- A line that needs a part is reported and never placed.
- A line with quantity 0 is not placed.
- It lays out on the geometry the sheet was drawn from (the stamped template, movable rooms where
  the sheet drew them, or the stamped room shape), never on what the intake says now.
- It MUST refuse when the generated base sheet is no longer on the design (replaced by a real
  plan, or deleted): "The generated plan was removed when the real plan was uploaded — restore
  the revision "Auto-saved before removing the generated plan" to fill equipment again." (or, when
  no revision can bring it back, a sentence telling the designer to place the equipment by hand).
- Every placed device carries an Auto tag: scope, row key and tier.

### 6.2 What is placed for a line

| Line | Placement |
| --- | --- |
| Priced by a catalog part | That part |
| Priced by an assembly | The virtual part `asm:<id>` |
| Priced by an allowance | The virtual part `allow:<row>:<tier>` |
| Not included, or needs a part | Nothing |
| Curtain row priced by a fabric part | One Grid curtain per unit: type from the row (Draw, Leg, Border, Full), named "<label> <n>", width = panel width × panels, height = finished height, the row's fullness, the mapped fabric |
| A row marked `lot`, or any row with quantity above 120 | One marker carrying the quantity |
| Any other row | One marker per unit |

### 6.3 The layout frame

All positions are fractions of the sheet: x from 0 at the left to 1 at the right, y from 0 at the
top (upstage) to 1 at the bottom (the house). The frame has three boxes, **stage** (S), **audience**
(A) and **booth** (B), plus optional extras:

| Plan | Stage | Audience | Booth | Extras |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Auditorium / PAC / Gym Stage template | The template's stage box at the opening | The House (or Gym Floor) | The Booth | Catwalk box and stage-edge curve (Auditorium) |
| Church template | The platform's core box (not its whole outline) | The Nave | The booth room, or the mix position | Side lights hang inside the stage box |
| Black Box | The room, inset (below) | The room | The Booth room | A "room" box that every point stays inside |
| Conference | The front platform | The room behind the platform | The Booth room | The same "room" box |
| Arena | The end stage, laid out as if at the top end | The floor | The Booth | A turn angle; a "lot box" |
| Any plan with no template (older designs) | x 0.20, y 0.12, w 0.60, h 0.28 | x 0.08, y 0.58, w 0.84, h 0.32 | x 0.38, y 0.44, w 0.24, h 0.09 | — |
| A sheet drawn from a room shape | The room with the stage role (for a proscenium family, narrowed to the opening) | The house-role room | The booth-role room | As the family above |

Black Box stage inset: 0.05 of the sheet's width in from each side wall, and at the house end by
room for the deepest line-array stack (`0.02 + 0.012 × 11 + 0.012` = 0.164), but never more than
40 % of the room's depth.

Arena: stage-relative positions are laid out as if the stage were at the top end, then turned
about the stage's centre by the stage's angle, in true proportions (y is scaled by the sheet's
height ÷ width before turning). Lot markers are then pulled inside the floor, inset past its
round corners by `0.3 × 13 ft`.

On a blackbox-family plan, every point except the Booth's rows (mixer / DSP, video processor,
projector) is pulled inside the room, 0.01 in from each wall.

### 6.4 Placement formulas

Helpers. `spread(n, x0, x1, y)`: n points on one row, the i-th at `x0 + (i + 0.5) / n × (x1 − x0)`.
`onRows(n, x0, x1, ys)`: n points dealt round-robin across the rows `ys`; with
`perRow = ceil(n / rows)`, point i is on row `i mod rows` in column `floor(i / rows)`, at
`x0 + (column + 0.5) / perRow × (x1 − x0)`. `E` = the design's electrics count (at least 1).
`sets` = the design's rigging set count.

| Row | Positions |
| --- | --- |
| Par, Automated | `onRows` across the stage's width on the electrics' lines. Electric j of E (j from 0) is at `S.y + (E − j) / (E + 1) × S.h` |
| Front | With a catwalk: `spread` along the catwalk from 6 % to 94 % of its width, at its mid-height. Otherwise `onRows` across the audience on two rows at 45 % and 60 % of its depth |
| Cyc | `spread` across the stage's width at `S.y + 0.05 × S.h` |
| Side light | Alternating left and right of the stage, 0.02 outside its side walls (inside them on a church, and never more than a quarter of the stage's width in). Pair k of `per = ceil(n / 2)` is at `S.y + (k + 0.5) / per × S.h` |
| Line-array loudspeaker | Alternating left and right, at `S.x − 0.03` and `S.x + S.w + 0.03`, stacked two to a row starting 0.02 below the stage and stepping 0.012 per row. Inside a room box the step closes up so the last row stays inside |
| Subwoofer | With a stage-edge curve: evenly along the curve by length. Otherwise `spread` across the stage at `S.y + S.h − 0.01` |
| Mixer / DSP, Video processor, Projector | `spread` across the booth at its mid-height |
| Screen | `spread` from 30 % to 70 % of the stage's width at `S.y + 0.1 × S.h` |
| Hoists (four kinds), Rigging point, Headblock | `onRows` across the stage's width on `min(n, max(1, sets))` set lines, line i of k at `S.y + (i + 0.5) / k × S.h` |
| Curtains | At the stage's centre x (Legs: `S.x − 0.03 × S.w`), at fractions of the stage's depth from the upstage wall: Draw 0.95, 0.50, 0.26; Full stage 0.50, 0.30; Border and Legs 0.74, 0.48, 0.22. When there are more curtains than fractions the list repeats, each repeat 0.04 further upstage, never closer to the upstage wall than 0.02 of the depth |
| Any other `each` row | `spread` across the stage (the audience, for an Audio row) at 0.03 above its lower edge |

Lot markers, per scope, the k-th lot of that scope (k from 0):

- Rigging: at the lock rail, `x = S.x + S.w + 0.02`, `y = S.y + (0.1 + 0.08 × k) × S.h`.
- Every other scope: `x = R.x + 0.06 + 0.07 × k`, `y = R.y + R.h − 0.03`, where R is the audience
  for Audio and the stage for the rest.

Every coordinate is clamped to 0–1.

## 7. Grid BOM, labor and the quote

### 7.1 The live BOM

The BOM panel prices one option. Lines come from seven sources:

| Source | Line |
| --- | --- |
| Devices | One line per part: quantity = the units placed (a lot adds its quantity) |
| Accessories | Catalog parts added under a heading, never placed (up to 200 per option; quantity up to 100,000) |
| Wires | One line per wire part: the measured feet of every run of that part, plus RiserLinks, rounded **up once** per part. Unmeasured runs are counted and shown apart |
| Curtains | One line per curtain |
| Custom items | Per-design items with a description, maker, model, unit cost, quantity and a system; priced like an allowance; never placed (up to 200 per option) |
| Labor | One line per heading (section 7.2) |
| Conduit | Priced conduit from the conduit riser (section 8.4) |

Lines are grouped under eight headings: Rigging, Curtains, Lighting, Audio, Video, Controls,
General and Conduit. A device's heading is its scope's, except that devices of three device types
— Control & Networking, Dimming & Power and Rigging Control — print under **Controls** (their
layer on the plan is unchanged). Unscoped devices print under General. An Auto-placed device
prints under its Auto scope. Within the device list the priciest line is first.

Accessories, custom items and labor overrides can be added under any heading except Conduit.

The BOM also shows the option's value, and (server-side only) cost and blended markup.

### 7.2 Labor

Labor is one line per heading that has material:

`labor = that heading's priced material × labor % × tier multiplier`

- "Material" is every non-labor line printed under the heading: devices, accessories, wires and
  RiserLinks, curtains and custom items.
- The tier is the option's Auto choice for that scope, else the option's own tier, else none
  (× 1.0). General always takes the option's tier.
- The designer may type a dollar amount per heading; it replaces the calculated amount. A typed
  $0 leaves the line off the quote. The typed amount is at most $10,000,000.
- Priced conduit carries no labor.
- Defaults: 18 % for every system; tier multipliers 1.00 / 1.15 / 1.30.

### 7.3 Create or update the quote

"Add to Quotes" on the Grid builds a draft quote from one option. The server prices it; nothing
priced by the screen is used.

Pricing rules:

- The customer's pricing tier is resolved from the design's contact, then the company, then Base.
- A catalog part sells at the tier price for that customer; a part with no tier price falls back
  to its list price and the line is flagged as a fallback.
- Assemblies and allowances placed by Auto are priced live at their real cost and then sold at
  cost × (1 + the customer's tier markup), like every other Grid part.
- Custom items sell at unit cost × (1 + tier markup). They are always priced, so they never make
  a design Incomplete.
- Curtains: sewn area × fabric rate × (1 + sewing %), then the tier markup.
- Labor lines carry the tier markup.

The quote MUST be refused, with the reason, when:

- the option has nothing to quote;
- any placed part is an unresolved seed placeholder;
- any virtual part no longer prices (named);
- the option's Auto choices still have a needs-a-part line (section 4.4);
- the conduit riser has a run it cannot price (named; section 8.4);
- the design is estimate-owned (section 5.16).

What is written: a draft quote with source "grid", the option's value, its markup
`(value − cost) ÷ cost`, the tier stamp, and a flat list of lines (SKU, description, quantity,
unit, unit price, extended price, flags for fallback and allowance, and a spec key where one
applies). The quote remembers the Grid project and option. A revision of the design is cut
automatically. Creating a quote again while the option's quote is still a draft updates it in
place.

Lines from a Grid quote print on no customer document by themselves; the estimator finishes the
quote in the Estimator (see chapter 13).

## 8. Risers

The Grid has three riser drawings. All of them follow one rule: devices, rooms and wires are
**derived from the plan** every time, so a riser can never drift from the layout. A saved riser
document holds only what the plan cannot know.

### 8.1 The derived riser

`/design/grid/<id>/riser` draws a one-line diagram:

- One node per Space that has devices or wire ends, plus one "Unassigned" node for devices and
  wire ends that sit in no space. Nothing disappears.
- Inside a node, one row per part with its unit count (a lot adds its quantity).
- A wire run is an edge from the space containing its first waypoint to the space containing its
  last waypoint.
- Editing a row's quantity on the riser edits the plan: without a lot in the row it adds or
  removes markers (newest first); with a lot it changes the newest lot's quantity, shrinks lots
  newest-first (never below 1) and only then removes markers. A lot may not pass 100,000 units.

### 8.2 The editable riser

The same page lets the designer arrange and annotate the riser. The saved document, per option,
holds:

| Item | Rule | Cap |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Node positions | A box per space (or Unassigned) on a 1,000 × 620 canvas; the riser may grow downward | — |
| Level lines | A label, an optional elevation and a height; draggable | 50 |
| Conduit annotations | Between two ends (a space or a device), with a label. **Never priced** | 500 |
| Riser notes | Numbered text | 100 |
| RiserLinks | A typed-length cable run between two ends, with a per-length part. Priced exactly like a wire | 1,000; up to 5,000 ft each |

Tools: + Device (adds a device to a space), + Space, Connect (creates a measured wire when both
ends are on one calibrated page, otherwise a RiserLink), Conduit, level lines, notes. The legend
is built from what is currently placed. A node's device row is 16 units tall (28 in Object mode,
to fit the riser drawing); a node's header is 24.

The server rebuilds every saved riser document into a canonical form and drops anything that
points at a deleted space, device or part. Copies of an option and revisions carry the document.

### 8.3 The conduit riser (lighting control)

`/design/grid/<id>/conduit-riser` draws a device-level riser in the format Peak's architect
partner uses: a **tag** per device, **conduit runs** between tags with a size label and signal
bubbles, level lines, stubs, notes and tables.

Device tag fields:

| Field | Limit | Default |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ID | — | The device's designator |
| Location | 24 chars | The space containing the device |
| P/D | P, D, P/D or blank | The part's tag default |
| Box | 4 chars | The part's tag default |
| Face | 8 chars | The part's tag default |
| Mount | 4 chars | The part's tag default |
| Height | 8 chars | The part's tag default |
| Power type | 2 chars | — |
| Power-controls contents | 60 chars | — |

A catalog part carries tag defaults; a placement overrides per field. Box, Face, Mount and Height
are upper-cased.

Which devices get a tag: every device at the end of a run, plus every device of the "always show"
device types even with no run — Control & Networking, Dimming & Power and Racks & Cases for the
lighting riser.

Suggestions: a plan wire (or RiserLink) joining two devices of the riser's system suggests a run
between them. Wires joining the same pair merge into one suggestion. A pair that already has a
run offers "joins run" for a wire not yet in it. Dismissing hides a pair until a new wire appears.
On the plan, drawing such a wire shows "Add to the lighting control riser?".

A run has two ends (a device or a stub), the wires inside it, a size (default 3/4"), a style
(conduit or cable management), an optional typed length (up to 5,000 ft; otherwise the measured
length of its wires), an optional lane position, and per-run price switches. A **stub** is a
labelled end that is not a device ("FA — wire pull by others"). A wire belongs to at most one
riser's conduit.

**Details** split the drawing: a detail has a number ("1", "1.1", "2"), a name and either every
space or a chosen list of spaces. The first is "Lighting control". Up to 20.

Layout is automatic and deterministic: level bands top to bottom; in each detail the head end
leftmost on its level with its runs leaving as a bundle of parallel vertical lanes; everything
else in hop order left to right; chains side by side. A tag or stub the designer drags is pinned
and the rest steps around it. Reset layout unpins everything. Runs are orthogonal; a run's only
free coordinate is its lane.

**Levels** are on the design: up to 30, each with a label (40 chars) and an optional elevation
(20 chars). A device's level is its space's level, else its sheet's default level.

Six tables print with the riser, each only when it has rows, in this order: power types, wire
legend, line legend, equipment rack contents (one per rack), power controls, box types. Text
prints upper-case. "Start from Bray's A–E" fills the power types table with five rows (in
`design-data.json` → `wireTypes.brayPowerTypes`).

Caps per riser: 20 details, 2,000 pinned tags, 200 stubs, 1,000 runs, 200 wires per run, 10
signals per run, 20 power types, 100 notes, 30 always-show types.

The page is read-only on a phone, fits the page (up to 1.5× on 24×36), has keyboard access, and
undo and redo across its own edits.

### 8.4 Conduit pricing and fill

**Pricing.** Each riser has two defaults, both **off**: price wire, price conduit. A run may
override either.

- Wire inside a run whose wire pricing is off is "by others": it leaves the priced wire lines and
  is listed, footage only, under "In conduit — by others". It is never on the quote.
- A run whose conduit is priced adds its conduit part by the foot. Sizes map to per-foot catalog
  parts in Estimating Rules → Conduit sizes. Footage is summed per part and rounded up once, and
  printed under the BOM's Conduit heading.
- A run that cannot be priced (no part for its size, no length) is a refusal sentence that names
  the run by its ends. Add to Quotes MUST refuse while any exist.
- An estimate-owned option prices nothing from a riser; the server MUST ignore price switches
  sent for one.

**Fill.** For each conduit run (not cable management) the program computes NEC fill for EMT:

- Each cable's area is `π × (outside diameter ÷ 2)²`. The outside diameter is a catalog field on
  the cable part.
- Allowed fill: 53 % for one cable, 31 % for two, 40 % for three or more.
- EMT internal areas in square inches: 1/2" 0.304; 3/4" 0.533; 1" 0.864; 1-1/4" 1.496; 1-1/2"
  2.036; 2" 3.356; 2-1/2" 5.858; 3" 8.846; 3-1/2" 11.545; 4" 14.753.
- The typed size is read loosely (`3/4`, `3/4"`, `3/4 in`, `1 1/4"`, `¾"`, `3/4"C`).
- Output: "Fill 28.4 % · suggests 3/4"", or "Overfilled — 3/4" allows 40 % for 5 cables · suggests
  1"", or "Fill unknown — no diameter for <cable>" when any cable has no diameter.
- Fill **suggests and warns only**. It MUST NOT set the size and MUST NOT block a quote or a
  print.

### 8.5 The A/V conduit riser

Each option may also have an A/V conduit riser, on the same engine (`?system=av` on the same
page). Differences from the lighting riser:

- It takes audio and video devices and wires.
- Signal bubbles and the wire legend are off by default.
- It has no power tables and no tag fields.
- Its first detail is "Audio/Visual". Its always-show types are Cable & Connectors, Displays &
  Projectors, Switching & Distribution, Networking, Racks & Cases and Assistive Listening.
- The plan's prompt picks the riser from the pair of devices a wire joins.
- Both risers feed the BOM: wire by others is the union, conduit footage sums across both.

### 8.6 DXF export

Each conduit riser downloads as a DXF file (`GET /api/grid/<id>/conduit-riser/dxf`, with
`?system=av` for the A/V riser):

- AutoCAD R12 ASCII, so every CAD package reads it.
- Units are sheet inches at 1:1, y up.
- Tags, signal bubbles, power diamonds and stubs are blocks with attributes, so a drafter can
  restyle a block or edit a value.
- Layers are named `PK-RISER-<LAYER>` for TAG, CONDUIT, CABLEMGMT, SIGNAL, LEVEL, TEXT and TABLE.
- Text is ASCII only, with the CAD codes for Ø and °; other characters become "?". At most 250
  characters per text.
- No title block.

## 9. Drawing set and schedules

### 9.1 The set

`/design/grid/<id>/set` prints a title-blocked drawing set for one option, at 11×17 (default) or
24×36, landscape. The 24×36 sheet is the 11×17 layout scaled by 36 ÷ 17: margins, title strip and
type all scale.

| Sheet | Number | Content |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cover | T-001 | Sheet index, symbol legend, general notes |
| Plan sheets | L-101…, A-101…, V-101…, R-101…, G-101… | One per drawing system per source page that has devices or wires of that system |
| System riser | E-501 | The riser of sections 8.1–8.2 |
| Lighting control riser | E-502, E-503… | One per page of section 8.3, with its tables |
| A/V conduit riser | continues after the lighting pages | Section 8.5 |
| Equipment schedules | E-601, E-602… | Section 9.3 |

Drawing systems: L Lighting plan (Lighting scope); A Audio plan; V Video plan; R Rigging & drapery
plan (Rigging and Curtains); G General devices plan (Unscoped). When a system has more than one
plan sheet, each title adds the source sheet's name and, past page 1, ", p. n".

Numbering rules:

- Numbers MUST be assigned before exclusions, so leaving a sheet out never renumbers another.
- The index and "n of N" count only the sheets that are included.
- The designer may exclude any sheet. All pages of the equipment schedules share one switch, and
  each conduit riser's pages share one.

A plan sheet draws the source page with only that system's devices and wires, at the design's
symbol size, each device labelled with its designator. Devices inside a holder are not drawn. A
key lists the designators. Labels are placed by trying a short fixed list of spots and taking the
first that hits nothing already on the sheet; the result is deterministic. Long designator lists
wrap, continue on "(cont.)" rows, or read "… see schedule"; they are never cut off.

The printed scale is `1" = <real length per printed inch>`, from the page's calibration and the
printed width. An uncalibrated page prints "NTS". A scale MUST never be guessed.

### 9.2 Title block

A strip 2.5 in wide (at 11×17) down the right edge, inside a 0.375 in margin. It carries:

- the company's name, address and phone (the office marked as the quote default, else the first)
  and logo;
- the project id, name, customer, venue and address;
- the option's name, only when the design has more than one option;
- the quote number, when the option has a quote;
- a revision table, newest first, at most 6 rows (letter, date, label), and a count of earlier
  ones;
- the status: "Rev <letter>", or "— Preliminary" for a design with no revision;
- drawn by (default: the design's creator) and checked by;
- the scale, the print date, the sheet number, title and "n of N".

Revisions are lettered A, B, … Z, AA, AB… in the order they were cut. A revision's label is its
own note, else a label typed in the set's settings, else "Design saved", "Issued with quote" or
"Earlier revision recalled".

Set settings saved on the design: size, drawn by, checked by (60 characters each), excluded
sheets, general notes (up to 4,000 characters; one note per line; a typed leading "1." is removed
because the sheet numbers them) and revision labels (80 characters). A design with no notes of
its own uses the standard general notes from Grid Settings.

### 9.3 Equipment schedule

`/design/grid/<id>/schedule` and the E-60x sheets show the same schedule. It has **no prices**; it
is the field document.

- One section per space (then Unassigned), each listing its devices by part: model number,
  description, quantity and designators. A holder prints on its own row with its members
  indented beneath it.
- A wire table: each wire run and RiserLink with its part, from, to and length.
- Wire footage per part, with a count of unmeasured runs.
- Parts print their model number, not an internal id.
- Optionally linked to a saved Lineset Builder design (the Lineset Builder stays in the app).

### 9.4 Rendering

The app prints the set through the browser, and also renders it to PDF on the server with headless
Chrome for client packages. A standalone program can rely on the browser's print dialog with a
page rule of one sheet per page, edge to edge.

## 10. Specs module

### 10.1 What it produces

The Specs module assembles a written specification in the three-part CSI layout — Part 1 General,
Part 2 Products, Part 3 Execution — as an editable Word file, **one CSI section per file**. It has
a library (the text) and a builder (one saved spec document per section per job).

### 10.2 The outline text engine

All library text is plain text, one item per line, indented two spaces per level (a tab counts as
one level; an odd number of spaces rounds down). One function turns that text into numbered
outline lines, and every preview and the Word writer use it, so they cannot disagree.

- Six levels, labelled `A.`, `1.`, `a.`, `1)`, `a)`, `(1)`. After Z comes AA.
- An article body starts at `A.`. A product entry body starts at `1.`, because the product's own
  letter used the first level.
- Two lines with the same indentation land at the same level. A deeper line goes down exactly one
  level, however far it is indented. The first line is always at the top level.
- Text deeper than six levels prints at level six, with a warning.
- A label the author already typed (`A.`, `1.`, `a)`, `(1)`) is removed. `1.1 ` is an article
  number, not a label, and is kept.
- Placeholders: `{{project.number}}`, `{{project.name}}`, `{{project.phase}}`,
  `{{project.issueDate}}`, `{{section.number}}`, `{{section.title}}`, and two lists,
  `{{articles}}` and `{{manufacturers}}`. A list placeholder alone on a line becomes one line per
  value, one level deeper; inline it becomes a comma list. A list with no values on its own line
  drops the line. An unknown or empty placeholder prints as written. Each case gives a warning.

### 10.3 The library

| Record | What it holds |
| --- | --- |
| Section | A CSI number (free text, e.g. "11 61 23"), a title, a sort order, Part 1 and Part 3 as lists of articles (title + outline body), a Part 2 style (paragraphs or table) and a quantities choice (on the drawings, or inline) |
| Part 2 article | Belongs to a section. A title ("Theatrical Stage Drapes"), acceptable manufacturers in order, an "A. General" outline (may use `{{manufacturers}}`), and the catalog categories that default into it |
| Authoring template | Per category: headings with guidance and a worked example, used to scaffold a new product's text |
| Curtain template | One per Grid curtain type, with slots filled from a curtain's size, fullness and fabric |
| Spec record | Section 10.4 |
| Catalog part spec fields | A part may carry its own spec title and body, an article pointer, a "same as" pointer to another part (one hop only; a chain or a cycle is an error), a sort order and a draft / approved state. These fields live on the catalog part and stay in the app |

Part 1 and Part 3 text may contain fill-in blanks written `[FILL IN: label]`.

The library exports and imports as one JSON file. Starter templates and curtain templates seed
themselves on a new database.

### 10.4 Spec records and the matching order

A spec record is one individually written specification:

- an id `PS-<section number without spaces>-<three or more digits>`, given by the server;
- a kind: catalog product, vendor product, system, or companion;
- a status: draft, ready or archived;
- its section and article, a title, a basis of design, a manufacturer;
- part numbers (up to 100; `#` in a part number is a wildcard);
- a match key (system records);
- "include with" (companion records: the ids it travels with);
- the spec text (an outline body), notes, a revision number, and who changed it when.

Every save that changes content writes the previous version to an append-only revision list.
Restore creates a new revision. There is one write path.

Uniqueness: one part number belongs to at most one **ready** record, and one match key to at most
one ready system record. A save that would break this is refused and names the other record.

**Matching one BOM row**, in order. The first step that gives an answer wins:

1. **Waived** — the row was waived on this spec, with a reason.
2. **Pinned** — the row names a record id. A ready record matches. A draft record reports "draft".
   An archived or unknown id falls through.
3. **Exact part number** — against ready records. The row's candidates are, in order: its own
   manufacturer number; its SKU (skipped when the SKU is a placeholder: blank, `CUSTOM`, `AI`,
   `CURTAIN` or anything starting `CRT-`); the SKU's text after a `Maker:` prefix; the linked
   catalog part's manufacturer part number; its model number. Comparison is upper-case with
   spaces collapsed.
4. **Wildcard** — a record's part number with `#`, where each `#` stands for exactly one digit
   from 1 to 5 and the lengths are equal.
5. **Match key** — the row's spec key against ready system records' match keys, compared in
   lower case with every kind of dash treated the same.
6. **Legacy catalog text** — if the row's catalog part has printable spec text of its own, that
   prints. A draft record that would have matched is mentioned as a note.
7. **Draft** — a draft record that matches by steps 3–5 is reported and not printed.
8. **No match** — with up to 4 candidate records, ranked by text similarity (a floor of 0.1), for
   a person to pick from.

At steps 3, 4 and 5, exactly one hit is a match; two or more is **ambiguous** and lists the
records. Only ready records print. Archived records never match. Nothing is ever assigned
automatically from a similarity score.

**Companions.** Every ready companion record whose "include with" names a matched record prints
once per document, under its own article, after that article's other entries.

### 10.5 The builder

`/design/specs` lists saved specs. A spec document has id `SP-####` (the first is `SP-1001`),
belongs to one section, and has a source: from scratch, from a quote, or from a Grid design.

- **Header**: project name, project number, phase (default "Construction Documents"), issue date
  and prepared by (200 characters each). Specs that share a project number are siblings, and a
  header change can be applied to all of them.
- **Parts 1 and 3** are read live from the library. Each `[FILL IN: …]` blank gets a form field
  (answers up to 500 characters). An answer whose blank has since left the library is flagged.
- **Products** come from the source's BOM, or are added by hand with a product picker (approved
  spec parts by default; "Show all catalog parts" lets the author write spec text onto a part on
  the spot; the picker returns up to 60).
- Each product row is matched (section 10.4) and shows its outcome with actions: **Link** to an
  existing record, **Write new** (saves a ready record), **Waive** (with a reason), **Pin**, and
  a choice between a project-only override of a record's text and an update to the library.
- Where a product lands in Part 2: its own article when that article belongs to this section;
  else a header chosen for it on this spec (never written to the part); else it is listed as
  belonging to another section, or as needing a header.
- A Part 2 entry whose title is the same as its article's title prints **flat**: no repeated
  heading, its clauses continuing the article's letters.
- Matched records print under their Part 2 article. Rows that matched the same record print
  once. Job values written `[like this]` inside a record get a form field each, keyed by the
  record id and the bracket's position.
- Waived rows print in a final article, **ITEMS NOT SPECIFIED**.
- A checklist names what was left out and why. It warns; it does not block.
- **Print quantities** is off by default. When on, quantities come from the BOM.
- Each download stamps the revision of every record it printed. The builder shows "Library
  changed since your last download" when a record has moved on.
- Saved specs read the library live; nothing is frozen until download.

Spec keys. A row may carry a spec key that names the system it belongs to (for example
"Stage Drapes – Main Curtain"). A Spec select sets it on Estimator custom and curtain lines, on
the custom-part form and in the Grid's curtain dialog. For a curtain with no key typed, one is
derived from its name, then its type; name keywords win ("Main Valance" is a valance).

Row identity, used to merge rows and to address a row action: a library-added row is the record
id; a real SKU is the SKU; a placeholder-SKU row with a manufacturer number is that number; else
the spec key plus description; else the description.

### 10.6 Word export

- Letter, 1 in margins, Times New Roman, body 10 pt, header and footer 11 pt.
- MasterSpec-style paragraph styles, a running header, and a footer "<section number> - <page>".
- Numbering is Word's own multi-level list: PART 1 → 1.1 → A. → 1. → a. → 1) → a) → (1), one list
  for the whole section. Labels are **not** typed in. A paragraph added in Word by picking a
  style numbers itself, and inserting or deleting one renumbers what follows.
- Part 2 as paragraphs, or as an equipment table (maker, model, description, optional quantity)
  when the section's style is "table".
- The section ends with an end-of-section line.
- Text is cleaned of characters Word's file format cannot carry.
- File name: `<project number>_<project name>_Spec <section number>_<section title>_<MM-DD-YYYY>.docx`,
  blanks dropped.
- An optional Peak letterhead at the top.

### 10.7 Library screens and import

`/design/specs/library` opens on spec records (filter by text, kind, section, status, maker); the
older section content is at `?view=sections`. There is a record editor with revision history, and
Import / Export as a spreadsheet in the layout of Peak's own spec-library workbook. "Include with"
cells are read loosely: any record id found in the cell counts. A catalog importer and a
product-specs template can also load spec text onto catalog parts by manufacturer number (matched
parts only).

## 11. Consulting

### 11.1 The engagement

A consulting engagement (`CE-####`) is a paid design job in which Peak is the specifier: Peak
designs and writes the specification, the job goes out to bid, Peak may bid its own spec, and the
engagement ends when construction administration ends. It is its own record, separate from
install projects.

An engagement holds: a name; the customer (the venue's organisation) and company id; venue ids; a
contact name; people with roles; the source quote id (none for a hand-made engagement); linked
budgetary design ids; an install quote id (a reference only — an engagement never converts into
an install); an architect (company and contact, plain text); a stage; a start and end date; the
disciplines bought; phases; milestones; decisions; meetings; submittals and RFIs; and documents.

**Stages**, in order: Proposal sent, Awarded, Design, Out to bid, Construction admin, Closed.
Every stage before Closed is "open". The stage is a free choice from a select; it orders and
labels, it never gates. Four older status words still on stored records map to the new ones:
active → Design, delivered → Out to bid, bid_supported → Construction admin, oversight_complete →
Closed. Anything unrecognised reads as Design.

**How an engagement is born and follows its quote.** For each consulting quote:

| Quote status | Engagement now | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sent | none | Create at Proposal sent |
| Won | none | Create at Awarded |
| Won | Proposal sent | Advance to Awarded |
| Lost | Proposal sent | Close |
| Sent (again) | Closed | Reopen at Proposal sent |
| anything else | anything else | Nothing |

A stage a person has moved past Proposal sent MUST never be changed by this rule. The rule is
idempotent. An engagement can also be created by hand, with a fixed fee or a milestone schedule,
and a proposal attached later.

### 11.2 Phases and the internal review

Default phases: Assessment, Schematic Design, Design Development, Final Documents, Bid Support,
Construction Oversight. The list is editable in Settings; a stored list replaces the defaults
whole. An engagement's phases come from the phases ticked on its proposal.

A phase has a name, a status (pending, active, complete), attachments, a checklist, review
comments, document markup and an internal review. Only Peak's own review gates progress; customer
approvals, architect comments and engineering stamps happen outside the app.

- **Checklist**: items are open, checked or waived. A waive MUST have a reason.
- **Comments**: itemised, each open or resolved, optionally anchored to a spot on a document.
- **Review**: submit, claim, approve, request changes. Approval MUST be refused while any
  checklist item is open or any comment is open.
- **Approval pin**: an approval records exactly which attachments and versions it approved, and a
  frozen copy is kept. The approval reads **stale** when the phase's attachments no longer match
  the pin (a revised drawing arrived afterwards). Stale is derived, never stored.
- **Markup**: annotations on a phase's documents (shapes, clouds, text, measurements) and a
  calibration per document page, the same model as the Grid's (section 5.3).

Phase reviews appear in the app's shared Reviews screen.

### 11.3 Milestones, decisions, meetings, submittals

- **Milestones**: a name, a target date (none = not scheduled), a completed date, an optional
  amount, and an optional phase. They seed from the proposal: one per scope (name = the scope's
  title, amount = its fee); else, for an older proposal with a milestone fee schedule, one per
  fee row; else none. Dated milestones with amounts feed a billing forecast in Reports.
- **Decisions**: who, when, the decision and its context.
- **Meetings**: when, attendees, minutes, linked decisions, an optional link to a recording, an
  optional phase, a title.
- **Submittals and RFIs**: kind, reference, received, responded, status (open, answered, closed),
  notes.

### 11.4 Tabs

The engagement page has eight tabs: Overview, Schedule, Activity, Phases, Milestones, Meetings,
Oversight (submittals and RFIs) and Documents. The Schedule tab (a Gantt generated from the start
and end dates and phase weights) and the Activity tab (notes, files and tasks in one capture) are
described in `project-management-spec.md` and are being removed with Project Management.

### 11.5 The consulting proposal

A consulting quote is built on its own screen (`/design/engagements/quote`). It carries:

- **Scopes**: a list of scope-of-work items, each a title, a description and a fee. The proposal
  total is the sum of the scope fees.
- **Phases** ticked from the phase list and **disciplines** ticked from a Settings list.
- **Assumptions**: ticked from a library of standard assumptions (ten ship as drafts; editable in
  Settings). The ticked texts are frozen on the proposal at save.
- **Terms**.
- The billing customer (often the architect) and, separately, the venue's organisation.
- An older proposal may instead carry a single fixed fee or a milestone fee schedule.

Saving a proposal logs it against an open lead for that customer, or creates one.

The proposal **document** (`/design/engagements/letter`) prints as a letter: a "Dear <contact>"
line, the scopes with their fees and the total, the assumptions as a bulleted list, the quote's
own terms, and an acceptance block for the customer's contact and Peak's estimator. It prints to
a clean PDF.

### 11.6 The D94 bid-spec generator

The older spec generator works from a BOM against spec text held on catalog parts. Saved outputs
are still viewable at `/design/engagements/spec/<id>`; new work uses the builder in chapter 10.

- Every BOM row lands in exactly one bucket: **ready** (the SKU matched a part with printable
  text), **no spec** (the part exists but has no text) or **no match** (the SKU is unknown; up to
  a few close parts are suggested by description similarity, never assigned).
- A row may be waived with a reason.
- The document cannot be finalised while any row is neither ready nor waived.
- Assembly groups ready rows into their sections, in order. A section with no parts is dropped.
  Waived rows are recorded and never rendered as specifications.
- Output is HTML that Word opens as an editable document, and a Word file with typed-in numbers
  (because these sections are pasted into someone else's project manual).
- The same generator still builds the specification file inside client packages and the
  Equipment Racks section (27 11 16) of a rack submittal. Those uses stay in the app.

### 12.2 Rules that cut across records

- A device's scope, the space it belongs to, a wire's length, a riser's nodes and a lot's
  designator range are **computed on read**, never stored.
- Every list inside a Grid project has a cap, and every write is checked on the server; the caps
  are in the sections above and in `design-data.json`.
- Catalog renames (a SKU changing to a model-number SKU) are followed in live records: Grid
  projects, Grid library entries, the Equipment map, conduit sizes and spec documents. Revisions
  are history and are not rewritten.

## 13. Doc-versus-code disagreements and known gaps

1. **Vocabulary size.** Project notes say the equipment vocabulary has 46 rows. The code has 51:
   the 46 equation items plus five Wire pull rows.
2. **Reference tier multipliers.** The tier table still carries cost and price multipliers per
   tier, and one total function still reads them. Every system priced from the Equipment map
   ignores them. They have no effect today.
3. **"Labor" in Controls.** The Controls row with key `controls:distro` is labelled "Labor" (it
   was "Distro system"). It is an equipment row priced from the map like any other, not the labor
   calculation.
4. **Gym Stage pipe length.** The Gym Stage's width is its stage opening, like a proscenium, but
   the pipe-length rule adds the 4 ft overhang only for the proscenium plan kind. A Gym Stage's
   pipe is therefore the opening with no overhang. The code comments record this as accepted.
5. **Drape width outside a proscenium.** For Church, Conference, Black Box and Arena, "width" is
   a room, platform or stage width, and drapes are still sized from it as if it were an opening.
   The code comments record this as an open question for Peak's owner.
6. **House depth in wire pull.** The wire-pull formula includes house depth, and no design form
   collects it — not even the house depth field of the templates. It always counts 0.
7. **Tier definitions are per browser.** The tier descriptions and default fabrics on Quick
   Design's Estimate view are remembered in the browser, not saved with the design or shared.
   Only the line-set counts are saved with a design.
8. **Grid quote lines on customer documents.** A Grid quote stores a flat list of lines. Project
   notes say these lines "print on no customer document — flagged, not fixed".
9. **The Equipment map starts empty.** Until an admin maps the rows, every Quick Design estimate
   and every Auto card is Incomplete. Project notes list the mapping as owner-gated work. A
   standalone program needs either built-in prices or the same mapping step.
10. **Room shape editor.** Only slice 1 is built. A shape cannot be reopened after the design is
    created, rooms cannot be added, and there is no DXF.
11. **Seed fabric rate.** The premium velour's seed rate is marked in the code as a placeholder.
12. **Consulting assumptions.** The ten shipped assumptions are marked in the code as drafts to
    be replaced from Peak's real letter.
13. **Other venue drawings.** Project notes list the arena corner radius and the Contemporary
    church's back-wall rule as awaiting the owner's review.
